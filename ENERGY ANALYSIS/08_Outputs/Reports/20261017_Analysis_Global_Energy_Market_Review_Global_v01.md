## Five things that matter

1. **The Hormuz shock dominates everything.** Since the Iran war began on 28 Feb, global oil supply has fallen 5.8 mb/d to 100.7 mb/d in 2026 (IEA), the largest loss on record. September brought a partial recovery (Saudi output back to 8.3 mb/d, Gulf crude exports at 75% or more of pre-war). Products, LPG and LNG are still at about half or less. Iran says the strait stays shut until its conditions are met, and talks are stalled.
2. **Demand is breaking.** Oil demand falls 2.6 mb/d in 2026, the first drop since 2020 (IEA). OPEC still shows +0.4 mb/d. EV sales are about 23 million (+15%, nearly 30% of cars), so part of the lost demand will not return.
3. **The real squeeze is diesel and LNG, not crude.** Diesel set records above $200/bbl. EU gas storage is only 71.6% full going into winter, Qatar LNG is running at about 20% of pre-crisis levels, and TTF peaked at $28/MMBtu. Europe and import-dependent Asia carry the cost, and North America is the relative winner.
4. **OPEC+ is fractured.** The UAE left on 1 May. OPEC+ produces 34.8 mb/d against 43.9 mb/d of capacity. Once Hormuz reopens, the spare capacity and lack of discipline point to a sharp price fall. The EIA has Brent at $74 by 4Q27, and the curve is steeply backwardated.
5. **Power is the structural story.** Renewables overtake coal in 2026, and wind plus solar beat gas globally in April. Data centres are driving the first system-wide capacity shortfall in PJM's history ($329/MW-day). Coal is getting a crisis bump, but the IEA and Ember see no large return to coal.
