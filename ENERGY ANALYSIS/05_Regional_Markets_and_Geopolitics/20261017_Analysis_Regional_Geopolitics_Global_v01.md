# 05 Regional Markets and Geopolitics: October 2026 brief

Prepared 2026-10-17. *Inferred* marks my own reading.

## Timeline of the 2026 shock
- **28 Feb:** US and Israeli strikes on Iran begin. Hormuz traffic collapses.
- **Week to 6 Mar:** WTI rises 35%, its biggest weekly gain since 1983.
- **18 Mar:** IEA announces a coordinated stock release.
- **8 Apr:** Pakistan-brokered ceasefire. Brent falls 13% in a day.
- **13 Apr:** US naval blockade of Iran begins.
- **30 Apr:** Brent reaches $126.
- **1 May:** UAE leaves OPEC and OPEC+.
- **June:** a reopening deal looks close. Tankers transit Hormuz and Brent falls to $73.74 on 24 Jun.
- **Mid-August:** the ceasefire ends and Hormuz traffic slows again (CNBC, 17 Aug).
- **About 11 Sep:** the Saudi East–West pipeline is struck. Brent spot hits $131 on 15 Sep.
- **October:** Iran says Hormuz stays closed until its conditions are met. Houthi attacks resume. US–Iran talks are stalled (Al Jazeera, 15 Oct).

## 05.1 OPEC and allies
- **The UAE exited OPEC and OPEC+ on 1 May 2026** after a quota dispute with Saudi Arabia. It had about 5 mb/d of capacity against a 3.5 mb/d quota, and it plans to run at full capacity once shipping allows.
- On 4 Oct, the seven core OPEC+ countries kept November targets at **31.01 mb/d** (Saudi Arabia 10.478, Russia 9.949, Iraq 4.431, Kuwait 2.676, Kazakhstan 1.628, Algeria 1.007, Oman 0.841).
- Actual OPEC+ output in September was **34.8 mb/d** against **43.9 mb/d sustainable capacity** (IEA). The gap is physical (Hormuz), not policy.
- *Inferred:* quotas are largely irrelevant while Hormuz is constrained. Once it reopens, the UAE's departure and the large stranded capacity point to a price war in 2027, which is consistent with EIA's $74 Brent in 4Q27.

## 05.2 North America
- **US rigs (9 Oct):** 603 in total (+56 y/y), including 462 oil rigs and 132 gas rigs, the highest since 2024. Canada has 222 rigs (+29 y/y).
- US crude output is 13.9 mb/d in 2026 and 14.3 mb/d in 2027 (EIA). US LNG exports are about 18 bcf/d.
- North America is the relative winner: it is a net exporter, has cheap Henry Hub gas (about $3.2) and strong refining margins. The Brent–WTI spread blew out to more than $15 in March.

## 05.3 Major demand hubs
- **Europe:** the most exposed region. TTF peaked at $28/MMBtu in mid-September, EU storage is at 71.6%, and wholesale power is about $105/MWh. Gas demand fell sharply in Q3 (residential and commercial −20%, industry −5%). The Russian LNG ban starts in 2027 and the pipeline gas ban on 30 Sep 2027.
- **China:** heavy stock draws (78% of the non-OECD crude draw in September) and more Russian crude. China supplies 55% of the top-five importers' purchases of Russian fossil fuels. Power demand is growing 5.5%, and renewables and coal are both cushioning the LNG shortfall. Its rare earth controls are a policy lever (suspension ends Nov-26).
- **India:** the fastest-growing economy (7.4% GDP), but inflation is rising to 5%. Its Russian crude imports fell 13% m/m in September as it pivoted to Middle East supply. Power demand is growing about 7%, with a peak of 270.8 GW in May.
- **Emerging economies:** Bangladesh, Pakistan, the Philippines and Thailand are switching to coal and cutting LNG purchases. Gulf domestic economies are hit directly by the war.

## 05.4 Russia and Caspian
- Russia earns about €625m/day from fossil exports (CREA, Sep-26) and benefits from high prices despite sanctions. Urals averaged $90.7. Shadow tankers carry 57% of seaborne Russian oil.
- Ukrainian drones are the main supply risk: Ust-Luga loadings fell 26% m/m in September, and Tuapse has not loaded a full cargo since June.
- Kazakhstan's OPEC+ target is 1.628 mb/d. CPC exports through the Black Sea should be watched for drone risk (*inferred*).

## 05.5 Latin America
- **Brazil, Guyana and Argentina** are the main sources of non-OPEC+ growth in 2026 (EIA). Guyana passed 900 kb/d after Yellowtail.
- **Venezuela:** 1.206 mb/d reported in September (1.150 mb/d by secondary sources), up from 0.924 mb/d in January. US licences are in place and 17 fields were handed to a US-backed operator in August. Merey crude averaged $93.75 in September.

## 05.6 Africa
- **Nigeria** has met its 1.5 mb/d crude quota for four months running (1.678 mb/d including condensate).
- **Mozambique LNG** (TotalEnergies, 12.9 Mtpa) restarted after force majeure was lifted in Nov-25. It is about 40% complete, with first LNG around 2029.
- *Inferred:* Atlantic-basin African cargoes earn large premiums while Gulf supply is out.

## 05.7 Gulf LNG and Qatar
- Qatar and UAE LNG is running at just over 2 bcm/month against more than 10 bcm/month before the crisis. QatarEnergy force majeure has been extended through November for some buyers. Energy Aspects expects about 38.7 Mt of Qatari exports in 2026, roughly half of capacity.
- North Field East Train 1 has slipped to 1H27 at the earliest. The disruption removed nearly 20% of global LNG supply (IEA).
- *Inferred:* buyers will push for contract diversification away from Hormuz-dependent supply. This structurally favours US, Canadian and African LNG final investment decisions; LNG Canada Phase 2 reached FID on 28 Sep.
