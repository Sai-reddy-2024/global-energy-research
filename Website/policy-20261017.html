06 Policy, Regulation and Carbon: October 2026 brief

An earlier edition. The latest brief supersedes it.

Prepared 2026-10-17. Inferred marks my own reading.

06.1 Climate accords and targets

  • COP31 takes place in Antalya, Türkiye, on 9–20 Nov 2026, with Murat Kurum presiding. Its three tracks are delivery of NDC 3.0, the COP29 finance goal (NCQG), and adaptation.
  • Inferred: the energy-security crisis splits the agenda. Importers are both speeding up renewables and EVs and delaying coal phase-outs (Germany, Italy to 2038, Japan, Korea and others). Expect energy security to frame the negotiations.

06.2 Carbon markets

  • EU ETS: the 12 Oct auction cleared at €86.28/t. The Q3 official price was €82.32, and the Q4-to-date average is about €84.76 (CBAM Guide's own calculation). EUAs are at their highest since January.
  • CBAM: the definitive phase began in 2026, with quarterly certificate prices (Q2 €75.28, Q3 €82.32). The Commission's July proposal (COM(2026) 616) would extend free allocation to 2040 and push the CBAM phase-in end to 2038. The Council aims for a position in December and a deal by Q1-27. Nothing has been adopted yet.
  • ETS2 (buildings and road transport) faces calls for suspension from Czechia and Italy. Inferred: high fuel prices make a 2027 start politically harder.

06.3 Subsidies and taxes

  • High prices are reviving consumer fuel support and windfall-tax pressure. The eight largest oil majors earned about $93bn in Q2-26, nearly double a year earlier, and President Trump said Exxon and Chevron were making "too much money".
  • No consolidated 2026 subsidy tally is available yet; pull the IEA fossil fuel subsidies database and IMF data when they update.
  • The US 45V hydrogen credit is still in place, but project cancellations continue (see 02.3).

06.4 Emissions data

  • Power-sector CO₂ is expected to rise about 1.1% in 2026 and fall about 0.5% in 2027 (IEA), as coal switching offsets record renewables.
  • US energy CO₂ is expected at 4.8 Gt in 2026, down 1.5% (EIA).
  • Inferred: global oil-related CO₂ should fall in 2026 because oil demand drops 2.6 mb/d. 2026 could therefore see a dip in total energy CO₂ for crisis reasons, not policy. Confirm with the IEA and Global Carbon Budget releases.
  • Flaring and methane: Gulf shut-ins and restarts may raise flaring. Check World Bank GGFR data.
