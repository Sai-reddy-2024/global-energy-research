06 Policy, Regulation and Carbon: November 2026 update

Published 10 Nov 2026

Prepared 2026-11-10. Updates the 17 Oct brief. Background that has not changed is in the v01 brief. Inferred marks my own reading.

06.1 Climate accords and targets

  • COP31 opened in Antalya on 9 Nov and runs to 20 Nov. The presidency's headline is "35 by 35": raise electricity's share of final energy from about 21% to 35% by 2035. The EU, UK and Canada support it, and Australia's Chris Bowen, President of Negotiations, backs it.
  • The pledge sits in the voluntary action agenda, not the negotiated text. Critics (E3G, Greenpeace) want it to specify clean electrification.
  • COP President Kurum said it is now clear the world will overshoot 1.5°C, and the summit will focus on implementation, not new targets.
  • Inferred: the Hormuz crisis lets the presidency sell electrification as energy security. Watch whether the cover decision mentions the transition away from fossil fuels.

06.2 Carbon markets

  • EU ETS: the Dec-26 EUA future traded at about €90–91 in the week to 5 Nov, its highest this year (energate). It eased to €86.66 on 7 Nov, and the market reference was €87.16 on 9 Nov.
  • The provisional Q4 CBAM certificate price is €86.57 (Carbon Pulse, 9 Nov). ABN AMRO expects a Q4 average of €86 and €80 for 2026.
  • Maritime shipping surrenders 100% of 2026 emissions in 2027, up from 70%.

06.3 Subsidies and taxes

  • No new data. US retail diesel at $6.13/gal (+60% y/y) keeps windfall-tax and fuel-relief pressure high (inferred).

06.4 Emissions data

  • EIA's state-level CO₂ series ends in 2022: US energy CO₂ was 4,934 Mt, with transport 1,840 Mt, electric power 1,532 Mt and industry 961 Mt. For 2026, use the STEO forecast (about 4.8 Gt, −1.5%) from the October brief.
  • Global power-sector CO₂ was flat in 2025 at 14.69 Gt (Ember), and carbon intensity fell to 460 g/kWh from 473. China's power emissions fell 1% and India's 3%, while the US rose 3% (gas burn). The sheet Power_CO2_Ember covers ten regions since 2010.
  • Inferred: 2025 looks like the plateau year for power emissions. The 2026 coal-for-LNG switching in Asia is the main risk to that.
