04 Downstream Refining and End Use: October 2026 brief

An earlier edition. The latest brief supersedes it.

Prepared 2026-10-17. Inferred marks my own reading.

04.1 Refining and processing

  • Global runs: 81.7 mb/d in 2026 (−2.3 mb/d), with a seasonal low of 81.3 mb/d in October and a rebound to 84.6 mb/d by year-end. Runs rise 2.8 mb/d in 2027 (IEA).
  • Diesel is the stress point. Gasoil output was 27.2 mb/d in 3Q26 (−1.7 mb/d y/y), and gasoil exports were down 10% over the first nine months. Diesel benchmarks set records above $200/bbl in September (about $220 in Europe and the US). European diesel cracks have since eased from about $100 to about $60/bbl (IEA).
  • Causes: the loss of Gulf product exports (1.9 vs 4.7 mb/d pre-war) plus Ukrainian drone damage to Russian refineries and ports (Tuapse, Novorossiysk, Ust-Luga).
  • Inferred: complex North American refiners with access to discounted crude and cheap gas have the strongest margins in the world right now. Refiners outside North America are squeezed by freight costs.
  • US retail diesel is forecast at $5.19/gal in 2026 and $4.49 in 2027 (EIA).

04.2 Power generation and grids

  • Global electricity demand is forecast to grow 3.6% in 2026 and 3.8% in 2027, reaching about 30,700 TWh in 2027. Growth by market in 2026: China 5.5%, India about 7%, US 1.8%, EU 2.1% (IEA mid-year).
  • Wholesale prices in H1-26: EU about $105/MWh (+15%), with H2 futures about 25% higher still. Japan rose more than 30% in Q2. The US was about $52 (+10%), while India (about $48) and Australia (about $47) were insulated.
  • US mix 2026 (EIA): gas 40%, nuclear 18%, coal 16%, wind 11%, solar 8%. PJM wholesale prices are up 41% in 2026.
  • Capacity markets: PJM cleared 2026/27 at $329.17/MW-day, against $28.92 two years earlier. The 2027/28 auction came in 6,623 MW short of the reliability requirement, the first system-wide shortfall in PJM's history, with a 14.8% reserve margin against a 20% target.

04.3 Demand sectors

Transportation

  • About 23 million EVs are expected to be sold in 2026 (+15%), close to 30% of global car sales. The oil shock is accelerating adoption (IEA via E&E News).
  • Oil demand is falling 2.6 mb/d in 2026, and diesel accounts for about 25% of the 4Q drop (IEA). Aviation and petrochemical feedstocks (LPG/naphtha) are hit by Gulf supply losses.

Industrial

  • European industrial gas demand fell about 5% y/y in Q3 (OIES). High TTF prices revive the de-industrialisation risk for chemicals, fertiliser and glass (inferred).

Residential and commercial

  • European residential and commercial gas use fell about 20% y/y in Q3, which is largely price-driven demand destruction (OIES).
  • In the US, EIA expects a mixed winter for heating costs: low gas prices but expensive heating oil.

Agriculture

  • Gas-based ammonia in Europe and Asia is squeezed by TTF around $24 and JKM around $25/MMBtu. The Gulf is a major urea and ammonia exporter, so the Hormuz closure tightens fertiliser markets (inferred; pull World Bank Pink Sheet urea prices next).

Data centres and AI

  • Data centres are the main driver of US power demand growth. US Q3 consumption rose 4% y/y, and EIA expects commercial demand growth of 2.8% in 2027.
  • PJM expects 5–7 GW per year of data centre load growth against only 2–3 GW per year of new supply through 2032. Virginia's data centre load was about 12.1 GW in 2025.
  • The IMF credits AI capex with offsetting the oil shock in 2026 GDP.

04.4 Energy storage

  • Battery deployment grew about 46% in 2025 to about 250 GWh, and battery costs fell 45% (Ember). BNEF forecasts about 158 GW of storage deployments in 2026.
  • Batteries helped Australia cut H1-26 wholesale prices by 30% (IEA).
