02.1 Fossil fuels

Crude oil

  • 2025 producers (EI): US 21.1 mb/d (21% of world, almost equal to Saudi Arabia plus Russia), Saudi Arabia 11.4, Russia 10.7, Canada 6.2, Iran 5.2, Iraq 4.4, China 4.3, UAE 4.2, Brazil ~4.3. Guyana reached 0.72 mb/d.
  • 2026 Gulf collapse and recovery (OPEC secondary sources): Saudi Arabia 10.1 mb/d in February, 6.8 in Q2, 8.0 in September. Kuwait 2.6 → 1.8; Iran 3.2 → 2.0 (US blockade). Aramco's total hydrocarbon output was 11.0 mmboe/d in Q3, against 12.9 for 2025.
  • Majors, 2025: ExxonMobil 4.74 Mboe/d (40-year high; Permian 1.6, Guyana >0.7 gross; proved reserves fell to 19.3 bn boe), Chevron 3.72 Mboe/d (+12%, record; >4.0 in Q4-25 after Hess; reserves 10.6 bn boe, RRR 158%), Petrobras 2.99 (RRR 175%), Shell 2.80, TotalEnergies 2.53, ConocoPhillips 2.38, BP 2.3, Equinor 2.14 (record), CNOOC 2.13.
  • Who gained and who lost in 2026: US shale-heavy and Atlantic Basin producers gained (Chevron US >2 Mboe/d for five quarters; Exxon Permian heading to ~1.8 Mboe/d). Companies with Qatar and Gulf exposure lost volume: ExxonMobil ~350 kboe/d, Shell Integrated Gas −17% in 9M26, ConocoPhillips Qatar, TotalEnergies ~5% of output.
  • Inferred: the winners are short-cycle onshore US and deepwater Americas. Higher prices are lifting earnings more than volumes: Exxon's Q3-26 earnings doubled while its production fell slightly.

Natural gas

  • 2025 producers (EI): US 1,074 bcm, Russia 609, Iran 265, China 264, Canada 206, Qatar 184, Australia 149, Saudi Arabia 134, Norway 121.
  • Qatar: LNG halted on 2 Mar; trains 4 and 6 (12.8 Mtpa) were damaged and need about three years to repair. The North Field East first train is ready, with first LNG expected in 1Q/1H27 (Reuters). QatarEnergy says undamaged trains can restart within weeks of Hormuz reopening.
  • US gas: Expand Energy is the largest producer (7.18 Bcfe/d in 2025; 7.45–7.55 guided for 2026). EQT is buying Range Resources for $13bn to reach >9 Bcfe/d and retake the top spot (closing 1Q27). Shell bought ARC Resources (Canada, ~370 kboe/d) in September.

Coal

  • 2025 producers (EI): China 4,663 Mt (52%), India 1,084, Indonesia 790, US 484, Australia 431, Russia 429.
  • Companies: Coal India 768 Mt in FY25-26 (down from 781), China Shenhua 332 Mt, Yankuang 182, Shaanxi Coal 175, Glencore 131 Mt (98 thermal + 33 steelmaking), Peabody 122 million short tons sold.
  • Inferred: the 2026 gas-to-coal switching in Asia benefits Indonesian and Australian exporters more than Coal India, whose output is falling.

02.2 Nuclear and hydro

  • Uranium: Kazatomprom produced 25,839 tU in 2025 (100% basis) and guides 27,500–29,000 tU for 2026; Kazakhstan supplies about 39% of world output. Cameco produced 21.0 Mlb (its share). Long-term uranium prices hit a record of about $96.50/lb at end-September.
  • Operators: Constellation, the largest US operator, generated 183 TWh of nuclear power in 2025 (94.7% capacity factor). EDF's French nuclear output rose to 373 TWh in 2025 (guide 350–370 TWh for 2026); Flamanville 3 reached full power in December 2025. Rosatom is building 27 of the world's ~63–66 reactors under construction.
  • There are 436 operable reactors worldwide (IAEA).

02.3 Renewables and alternatives

  • Developers: SPIC (~94 GW renewable), China Three Gorges (~125 GW clean energy), Iberdrola (46 GW renewable of 58 GW), Brookfield (~46 GW), NextEra Energy Resources (~33 GW plus 3 GW storage). Capacity figures use different definitions, so treat them as indicative.
  • Manufacturing: the top four module makers (LONGi 87 GWp, Jinko 86, JA 70, Trina 67) all lost money on near-cost pricing. All five of the top wind OEMs by 2025 installations were Chinese (Goldwind 29.7 GW); Vestas led outside China (12.9 GW).
  • Outlook (IEA Renewables 2026, 6 Nov): ~4,800 GW of new renewable capacity in 2026–31, solar over 75%. China's solar additions fell 55% y/y in 9M26 after its move to auctions, so global solar additions are forecast to fall 11% in 2026, the first drop in 21 years.

02.4 Production costs

  • US shale breakevens (Dallas Fed, Mar 2026): a new well needs $66/bbl WTI on average (Delaware $63, Midland $69, large firms $59), and existing wells cover operating costs at $43. Midland's new-well breakeven has risen 49% since 2017.
  • Executives' price view (Q3-26): WTI at $88 at end-2026, $79 in two years and $82 in five. Firms budget on $65–70 because of steep backwardation, and costs are rising fast.
  • Power (Lazard LCOE+ 2026): unsubsidised utility solar costs $40–98/MWh (+18% y/y), onshore wind $37–99, gas combined cycle $51–129 (a 15-year high) and new nuclear $175–255.
  • Inferred: at Brent near $90, every major basin is profitable, but a fall to the mid-$60s (the reopening case by 2027) would push US new-well economics to breakeven and slow shale growth.

02.5 Critical minerals

  • Copper, 2025: BHP 1.46 Mt (equity), Codelco 1.41, Freeport 1.53 (down after the Grasberg mud rush), Southern Copper 0.96, Zijin 0.89–1.09, Glencore 0.85, CMOC 0.74.
  • Lithium: world mine output rose 31% to ~290 kt Li in 2025 (USGS), led by Australia (92 kt), China (62) and Chile (56). SQM (~233–260 kt LCE) and Albemarle (~235 kt LCE) lead.
  • Concentration (USGS 2026): DRC 74% of cobalt, Indonesia 67% of nickel, China 69% of rare earths and 78% of graphite. China's rare earth export-control suspension ends this month (November 2026), the main near-term risk.
