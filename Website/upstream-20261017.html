02 Upstream Production by Source: October 2026 brief

An earlier edition. The latest brief supersedes it.

Prepared 2026-10-17. Figures are as published; inferred marks my own reading.

02.1 Fossil fuels

Crude oil

  • Global supply in 2026 is 100.7 mb/d, down 5.8 mb/d (IEA), the biggest annual loss on record. Supply bottomed at 96.4 mb/d in 2Q26 and is recovering to 101.4 mb/d in 2H26. September supply rose 2.5 mb/d m/m to 102.3 mb/d.
  • Gulf shut-ins peaked at 10.9 mb/d in May, then fell to 5.8 mb/d in August and 4.8 mb/d in September (EIA).
  • Saudi Arabia produced 8.3 mb/d in September, up from 5.97 in August, as exports rerouted. Iraq produced 4.10 mb/d and Kuwait fell to 1.49 mb/d (IEA).
  • United States: 13.7 mb/d (2025), 13.9 mb/d (2026e), 14.3 mb/d (2027e) (EIA). Higher prices are adding rigs, with 462 oil rigs on 9 Oct (see 05.2).
  • Atlantic Basin growth in Guyana, Brazil and Argentina is the main non-Gulf offset (see 05.5).
  • Inferred: unconventional short-cycle supply in the US and Argentina is the only lever that responds within months. Deepwater (Brazil, Guyana) is already scheduled and not price-responsive in 2026.

Natural gas

  • Qatar LNG was down 20.8 bcm y/y in Q3, while the rest of the world added 13.5 bcm, for a net global LNG loss of 7.3 bcm (OIES).
  • North American LNG exports reached 47.3 bcm in Q3, up 22% y/y (OIES). EIA forecasts US gross LNG exports of 15 bcf/d (2025), 18 bcf/d (2026) and 19 bcf/d (2027).
  • Associated gas: Gulf oil shut-ins also cut associated gas. This affects Gulf domestic power and petrochemical feedstock (inferred, not quantified in sources).

Coal

  • Gas-to-coal switching is under way in Asia and Europe. At least eight countries (Japan, South Korea, Bangladesh, the Philippines, Thailand, Pakistan, Germany and Italy) have announced more coal burn or delayed phase-outs.
  • Ember's upper-bound estimate is +175 TWh (+1.8%) of coal power in 2026, but data so far shows no large "return to coal". The IEA expects coal generation to rise about 1.4% in 2026.
  • US power-sector coal stocks were about 100 Mst at end-September (EIA).

02.2 Nuclear and hydro

  • The IEA forecasts global nuclear output up only about 0.6% in 2026, then +4.6% in 2027 to about 3,003 TWh, a record. US nuclear output is at its highest since 2019.
  • US restarts include Palisades (800 MW, Holtec, DOE $1.52bn loan guarantee) and Crane/TMI-1 (835 MW, Constellation–Microsoft, targeted for 2027–28). Current operating status should be checked against NRC and Holtec before relying on it, because the sources I found are dated.
  • Inferred: the LNG shock strengthens the energy-security case for nuclear in Japan, Korea and Europe.

02.3 Renewables and alternatives

Solar

  • 2025 was a record year: solar generation rose 636 TWh (+30%) to 2,778 TWh, and about 647 GW of solar was added (Ember). Solar overtook wind for the first time.
  • In April 2026, wind plus solar generated more electricity than gas worldwide for the first time (Ember).

Wind

  • Ember expects wind plus solar to pass nuclear in 2026. Renewables overall are set to overtake coal in 2026 (IEA), rising from 33% of generation in 2025 to 37% in 2027.

Hydrogen

  • The sector is still correcting. More than 50 green hydrogen projects have been cancelled since 2021 (Fortescue, Air Products and others), and the offtake gap remains the binding constraint.
  • Inferred: higher gas prices improve the economics of green relative to gray hydrogen outside the US. That does not overcome the lack of buyers in 2026.

Biomass and biofuels

  • High diesel prices favour biodiesel and HVO margins. No new consolidated 2026 data was found; pull IEA Renewables and USDA data next.

02.4 Production costs

  • With Brent averaging about $96 in 2026 (EIA), nearly all producing basins are profitable. The binding cost is now delivered cost: VLCC earnings reached about $723k/day on 18 Sep (see 03.1).
  • To add: Dallas Fed Energy Survey Q3-26 breakevens and Lazard LCOE 2026.

02.5 Critical minerals (IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026)

  • The average top-refiner share for key minerals (excluding rare earths) rose to 72% in 2025 from 70% in 2023. China leads most minerals and Indonesia leads nickel. China holds about 50% of global copper smelting capacity.
  • Prices from Jan-25 to Apr-26: base metals rose about one-third, with record copper. Lithium more than doubled, cobalt rose about 130% (DRC export quota) and tungsten rose 6x.
  • Export controls: China's mineral tariff codes under control have tripled since 2023. The expanded Oct-2025 rare earth controls are suspended only until November 2026, a near-term risk to watch. The IEA estimates about $6.5tn of downstream output outside China is exposed.
  • Copper's 2035 deficit has narrowed from about 30% to 25%. Lithium deficits persist, and a new cobalt gap has opened.
